Solar Geoengineering

What has actually happened? A sourced look at outdoor experiments, private-sector activity, government oversight, and what remains unknown.

Working article · September 2026 · Daily Sunwatch Bulletin

Documented Research Hypothesis Unknown / unresolved
Evidence first. This article distinguishes documented activity from research, interpretation, and unresolved questions. External links are provided for source review and do not imply affiliation or endorsement.

Solar radiation modification (SRM), sometimes called solar geoengineering, is often discussed as a future technology. The public record is more nuanced: large-scale operational deployment has not been publicly documented, while limited outdoor experiments and intentional releases related to solar geoengineering have occurred.

The distinction matters: “no large-scale deployment” is not the same statement as “no outdoor solar-geoengineering activity has ever occurred.”

What the public record establishes

Documented

EPA: large-scale deployment vs. limited activity

EPA distinguishes large-scale solar-geoengineering deployment from limited private-sector activity and discusses known U.S. outdoor activity on its geoengineering pages.

Documented

Make Sunsets: intentional sulfur dioxide releases

EPA reported that, as of May 2025, Make Sunsets said it had released about 0.1 tons of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) into the stratosphere. Make Sunsets publicly describes its balloon-based activity and sells “Cooling Credits” tied to SO₂ delivery.

Documented

University of Washington: marine cloud brightening field test

EPA describes a University of Washington-led experiment that sprayed saltwater aerosol from the retired USS Hornet in Alameda, California. EPA says the experiment ran for about 20 minutes before local officials stopped it.

EPA further says later-revealed documents indicated the test may have been a precursor to a proposed experiment involving an area of roughly 3,900 square miles over ocean—an aspect EPA says had not previously been disclosed to the public.

Documented

GAO: federal weather-modification reporting has weaknesses

In February 2026, the U.S. Government Accountability Office reported weaknesses in NOAA's weather-modification reporting and oversight system and recommended improvements intended to provide more reliable and useful public information.

GAO-26-108013 — Weather Modification ↗
Documented

NOAA: weather modification is a reportable activity

The Weather Modification Reporting Act requires covered weather-modification activities in the United States to be reported to the federal government. NOAA administers the reporting program and maintains historical weather-modification records.

Research / development

Stardust: sunlight-reflection technology under development

Stardust says it is developing an end-to-end sunlight-reflection technology using proprietary particles. The company says its technology has not been deployed and describes government authorization, independent validation, and governance as prerequisites for future deployment.

What this does not establish

The existence of documented SRM experiments, releases, companies, and regulatory gaps does not by itself identify the composition or purpose of a particular aircraft trail seen from the ground. Persistent contrail cirrus is sufficient to explain many long-lasting, spreading aircraft trails. Conversely, a government statement about the absence of large-scale deployment is not a chemical analysis of any particular observed trail.

Those are separate evidentiary questions. Bulletin will treat them separately.

Governance questions

The emerging issue is not only whether SRM can work, but who may conduct outdoor experiments, what must be disclosed, what environmental review is appropriate, how affected communities participate, how activities are reported, and who is responsible for unintended consequences. These remain active scientific, legal, ethical, and public-policy questions.
