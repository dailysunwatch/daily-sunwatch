Solar Geoengineering

What has actually happened? A sourced look at outdoor experiments, private-sector activity, government oversight, and what remains unknown.

September 2026 · Daily Sunwatch Bulletin

Overview

Where research ends and deployment begins.

Solar radiation modification (SRM), sometimes called solar geoengineering, is often discussed as a future technology. The public record is more nuanced. Large-scale operational deployment has not been publicly documented, while limited outdoor experiments and intentional releases related to solar geoengineering have occurred.

“No large-scale deployment” is not the same statement as “no outdoor solar-geoengineering activity has occurred.”

Government Oversight

GAO: NOAA should strengthen oversight.

Weather Modification: NOAA Should Strengthen Oversight to Ensure Reliable Information

In February 2026, the U.S. Government Accountability Office reported that NOAA was not fully meeting its responsibilities to maintain and share useful, complete, and transparent weather-modification reports. GAO estimated that over half of reports filed with NOAA likely contained errors, including missing required information, and found that NOAA's forms and processes were not well-suited to emerging solar geoengineering activities.

U.S. EPA

What EPA says about solar geoengineering.

Solar Geoengineering: Frequent Questions

EPA says large-scale solar geoengineering deployment has not happened and that the U.S. government is not conducting outdoor SRM testing or large-scale deployment. On the same page, EPA discusses limited private-sector activity, the Make Sunsets releases, the University of Washington marine-cloud-brightening experiment, state laws, international governance, and the distinction between geoengineering and weather modification.

University of Washington marine cloud brightening experiment

EPA says a University of Washington-led experiment sprayed saltwater aerosol from the retired USS Hornet in Alameda, California, for about 20 minutes before local officials stopped it. EPA also says later-revealed documents suggested the test may have preceded a proposed experiment involving roughly 3,900 square miles over ocean.

Private Sector

Companies developing and conducting SRM activity.

Make Sunsets

EPA reported that, as of May 2025, Make Sunsets said it had released about 0.1 tons of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) into the stratosphere. The company publicly describes balloon launches and sells “Cooling Credits” tied to SO₂ delivery.

Make Sunsets — Company website ↗  ·  EPA — Government Action ↗

Stardust

Stardust says it is developing an end-to-end sunlight-reflection technology using proprietary particles. Its FAQ says its technology has not been deployed and describes government authorization, independent validation, and governance as prerequisites for future deployment.
