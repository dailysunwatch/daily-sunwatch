Sources & Resources

Where Sunny gets the data. ☀

Daily Sunwatch draws on established scientific and government resources for solar, space-weather, lunar, and terrestrial weather information. This page highlights the main sources behind the project without getting into Sunny's entire internal workflow.

Each title links to an official resource. Data, imagery, forecasts, and model guidance remain the work of their respective providers.

Scientific Sources

Observations, models & forecasts

The primary scientific resources that help Daily Sunwatch keep an eye on the Sun, near-Earth space, the Moon, and occasionally the weather down here too.

NOAA
SWPC

NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center ↗

Operational U.S. space-weather observations, forecasts, alerts, and imagery.

NOAA SWPC is one of Daily Sunwatch's primary operational sources, providing much of the real-time context behind Sunny's solar, solar-wind, geomagnetic, aurora, and coronagraph observations.

Solar activity Space weather Geomagnetic conditions Coronagraph imagery
Visit NOAA SWPC ↗
NASA
SDO

NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory ↗

Near-continuous, multi-wavelength observations of the Sun.

NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory provides high-resolution solar imagery used throughout Daily Sunwatch for routine observations, flare context, multi-wavelength views, and visual analysis.

Solar imagery Multi-wavelength views Flare context
Visit NASA SDO ↗
NASA
DONKI

NASA CCMC / DONKI ↗

NASA's space-weather event database and notification resource.

DONKI provides event information and scientific context for activity such as solar flares and coronal mass ejections, helping Sunny connect observations to reported space-weather events.

Solar flares CMEs Event context
Visit DONKI ↗
WSA–
ENLIL

WSA–ENLIL ↗

Physics-based modeling of the solar wind and CME propagation through the inner heliosphere.

Daily Sunwatch uses WSA–ENLIL model guidance for additional context when evaluating how reported CME events may propagate through interplanetary space and toward Earth.

CME modeling Propagation Earth-impact context
Model guidance is presented as guidance. Sunny does not independently calculate CME impact.
USNO

U.S. Naval Observatory — Astronomical Applications ↗

Official astronomical data from the U.S. Naval Observatory.

Daily Sunwatch uses USNO astronomical data to support its nighttime content, providing lunar phase, illumination, and upcoming phase information.

Moon phase Lunar illumination Phase timing
Visit USNO ↗
NASA
SVS

NASA Scientific Visualization Studio ↗

NASA Goddard scientific visualizations and imagery.

NASA SVS provides lunar visualization imagery used in Daily Sunwatch's nighttime content, complementing astronomical information from the U.S. Naval Observatory.

Lunar imagery Nighttime observations
Visit NASA SVS ↗
NWS

National Weather Service ↗

Official U.S. terrestrial weather forecasts and observations.

The National Weather Service provides terrestrial weather information for Daily Sunwatch's occasional East Coast weather observations.

Terrestrial weather East Coast forecasts
Visit weather.gov ↗
Visual Analysis

Sunny Vision

An optional supporting layer for selected imagery, kept separate from Sunny's official scientific data sources.

SUNNY
VISION

Sunny Vision

Optional AI-assisted visual analysis for selected solar imagery.

Sunny Vision helps Daily Sunwatch examine selected imagery for potentially notable visible features and add short observational context when appropriate. It is supplementary rather than authoritative, and official scientific observations and model guidance remain separate.

Image interpretation Supplementary context Conservative analysis
Sunny Vision uses OpenAI technology. It is not used as a replacement for official NOAA, NASA, USNO, or NWS data and guidance.
